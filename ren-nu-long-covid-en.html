Section 02

The Ketogenic Metabolic Therapy (KMT) Program for Long COVID from Santa Barbara Nutrients is a guided program designed to support metabolic and immune health through science-informed nutritional changes. Originally developed as the Ren-Nu™ program for polycystic kidney disease, this adapted program is now being offered to people living with Long COVID (post-COVID condition) who are interested in a structured, dietitian-supported ketogenic nutrition approach.

Section 03

Why This Program Exists

This program grew out of personal experience. Dr. Dana Dharmakaya Colgan, a neuropsychologist and neuroscientist who developed Long COVID herself, found meaningful benefit from a ketogenic metabolic approach during her own Long COVID recovery. That experience led her to partner with Santa Barbara Nutrients to develop this KMT program specifically for Long COVID.

Dr. Colgan has since built a research program studying ketogenic metabolic therapy for Long COVID and related post-viral illness, and has published several studies together with the Santa Barbara Nutrients team. See our Long COVID studies below.

Long COVID is thought to involve ongoing inflammation and problems with how the body makes and uses energy. The body usually runs on sugar (glucose) for fuel, but when a person eats very few carbohydrates, it switches to burning fat instead and makes a clean, backup fuel called ketones. Being in “ketosis” simply means your body is running on ketones rather than sugar. The brain, muscles, and even the immune cells can use ketones for energy. Early research suggests ketones may help calm inflammation, support the immune system, and provide the brain and other organs with a more efficient energy source.

Other ketogenic programs for Long COVID exist. We set out to build one that is more closely grounded in research, more affordable, fully remote and doable from home, and able to reach more people.

Section 05

Is This Program Right for You?

  • You have been diagnosed with Long COVID / post-COVID condition, or have symptoms persisting at least 2–3 months after infection that are not explained by another diagnosis
  • You have discussed dietary change with your treating clinician and have no contraindication to a ketogenic diet
  • You are motivated to make meaningful changes to your diet and lifestyle
  • Your own physician will continue to oversee your care while you participate. This program complements, and does not replace, standard medical care.

Important safety note: A ketogenic approach is not appropriate for everyone. It should be used with caution or avoided in people with type 1 diabetes, those on insulin or other glucose-lowering medications that can cause hypoglycemia, pregnancy, a history of eating disorders, or certain metabolic conditions. Discuss eligibility with your physician before enrolling. We will also review eligibility with you during your discovery call.

Section 08

What the Science Does — and Does Not — Yet Show

We believe in being transparent about the evidence.

Why we think ketones might help ＋

Early laboratory and clinical studies point to a few different ways ketones may be useful. First, they seem to give immune cells better fuel: in the lab, immune cells called T cells worked harder and lasted longer when they had ketones (especially BHB) to burn, which may help the body respond to infection more effectively (Karagiannis et al., Nature, 2022). Second, those same studies found that people with severe COVID-19 often have trouble making ketones on their own, so the illness may leave the body short on a fuel it needs to recover, and adding ketones through diet or a supplement may help make up the difference (Karagiannis et al., Nature, 2022). Third, ketones appear to help “turn down” some of the inflammation that drives many Long COVID symptoms.

Several independent research reviews have looked at this evidence and concluded there are sound scientific reasons to explore ketones as a way to support the body during and after COVID-19 (Bradshaw et al., Oxidative Medicine and Cellular Longevity, 2020; Bolesławska et al., Viruses, 2023). And in one small study, people who fasted on a set schedule and cut out added sugar reported improvement in some Long COVID symptoms (Bunker et al., Scientific Reports, 2025). Taken together, these findings are the reason we think this approach is worth studying.

Our Long COVID studies

Collectively, our preliminary studies assess feasibility, acceptability, patient experience, and case-level outcomes. Results from these four studies demonstrate consistent signals of tolerability, high satisfaction, and preliminary symptom improvement. However, these findings derive from small, uncontrolled samples that relied largely on self-reported data, enrolled predominantly female participants, and lacked a comparison group.

1. Proof-of-concept single-arm feasibility trialn=11 · in peer review ＋
Design
National, fully decentralized, single-arm, 12-week proof-of-concept study of a fully remote Ketogenic Metabolic Therapy (KMT) intervention in adults meeting criteria for Long COVID, with a specific phenotype of post-infectious dysautonomia and ME/CFS.
Primary aim
Feasibility and acceptability (enrollment, retention, adherence, satisfaction, safety), not efficacy. Symptoms and functioning measured at baseline, post-intervention, and 3-month follow-up.
Results
11 participants across 6 US states completed the survey. Predetermined feasibility and acceptability benchmarks were met. All participants achieved and maintained nutritional ketosis. Participants reported clinically meaningful improvements in fatigue, mood, social functioning, sleep, autonomic regulation, life satisfaction, and post-exertional malaise, most maintained at 3 months.
Conclusion
The remote KMT was feasible, acceptable, and safe. Preliminary improvement signals support larger controlled trials. Key limits: very small n, single-arm, self-report, no control group.

Reference: Registered on ClinicalTrials.gov as NCT07745699. Results manuscript currently in peer review.

2. Designing Nutrition Studies for Long COVID and Related Infection-Associated Chronic Illness: Qualitative Insights From a Patient-Reported Evaluation of Ketogenic Metabolic Therapyn=41 ＋
Design
Cross-sectional, mixed-methods survey of adults who completed a 12-week virtual program integrating ketogenic metabolic therapy and lifestyle interventions (KMT-LS). Recruitment emails went to 194 participants; 41 responded. Quantitative data summarized descriptively; qualitative data via conventional content analysis.
Population
Mean age 41, 90% female; diagnoses included post-viral dysautonomia, ME/CFS, and Long COVID.
Results: four qualitative themes
  • Program benefits included a sense of community, validation of medical illness, increased health efficacy, the start of a healing journey, and the importance of a multicomponent program.
  • Program challenges included the time and effort to participate when not feeling well, information overload, trusting the program after so many failed interventions, and the nonlinear nature of metabolic adaptation.
  • Program recommendations included increased 1:1 support, a longer treatment period, and more post-program supports.
  • Scientific considerations included measuring endpoints for at least a year post-program, assessing functional capacity before and after the program, and conducting responder stratification and baseline characterization to understand who benefited and why.
Conclusion
Future nutrition trials for infection-associated chronic illness should use longer intervention periods, multicomponent design, patient-centered outcomes, lived-experience integration, and tailored support. This is hypothesis-generating design guidance, not an outcome study.

Reference: Colgan DD, et al. Journal of Patient Experience, 2026. doi:10.1177/23743735261442541

3. Telehealth-Based Ketogenic Metabolic Therapy With Lifestyle Interventions for Post-viral Illness: A Research Brief of Patients’ Experiencesn=41 · international ＋
Design
Cross-sectional survey characterizing patient-reported experiences with a fully remote intervention combining ketogenic metabolic therapy and thiamine supplementation with behavioral strategies (circadian entrainment, mindfulness-based resilience coaching). Focus on feasibility, acceptability, and program optimization (preferred duration, dosing, structure).
Results
Among 41 international respondents, 96% found it helpful, 96% would recommend it, and 75% felt “a lot better” after completion.
Conclusion
Patient input supports refining this intervention. Results show strong feasibility and acceptability signals to justify larger controlled efficacy/mechanistic trials. Limits: self-selected respondents, self-report, no control, satisfaction-oriented endpoints.

Reference: Colgan DD, et al. Journal of Patient Experience, 2026. doi:10.1177/23743735261459218

4. Clinically Meaningful Improvements in Long COVID Symptoms Following Ketogenic Metabolic Therapy Combined with Lifestyle Interventions — A Clinical Case Report and Review of the Literaturecase report ＋
Design
Case report of one patient with Long COVID treated with remotely delivered ketogenic metabolic therapy and lifestyle interventions (KMT-LS), with a supporting literature review.
Results
The participant reported clinically meaningful improvement across multiple Long COVID symptoms.

Reference: Colgan DD, et al. Case Reports in Clinical Medicine, 2025;14:391–410. doi:10.4236/crcm.2025.148052

What we don’t know yet: No large, high-quality study has proven that a ketogenic diet or ketone supplement actually treats Long COVID. Scientific studies on diet, nutrition, and Long COVID are small or early (Bradbury et al., Journal of Integrative and Complementary Medicine, 2023; Zeraatkar et al., BMJ, 2024). This program is a nutrition education program. It is not a medical treatment, has not been approved by the FDA to treat, cure, or prevent Long COVID or any disease, and is not a substitute for care from your physician.
